Q — Does metformin reduce cardiovascular risk in non-diabetic patients?

Evidence is mixed. Trials in insulin-resistant, non-diabetic adults show modest reductions in cardiovascular event markers1, but effect sizes are smaller than in diabetic cohorts and vary by baseline risk2. Two studies report no significant benefit over lifestyle intervention alone3.

EVIDENCE STRENGTH
1Lancet Diabetes Endocrinol., cohort study, n=2,414
2J Am Coll Cardiol., meta-analysis, 9 trials
3Diabetes Care, randomized trial, n=683
01 — Why retrieval

A language model's memory is not a literature review.

Generated answers drift from what was actually published. Retrieval keeps the system anchored to sources you can check yourself.

Asking a model from memory

Confident wording, no way to trace a claim back to a study — and no signal when the field genuinely disagrees.

  • Answers can't be traced to a source
  • No indication of study quality
  • Conflicting findings get flattened into one answer
  • Training data goes stale

Asking MedResearch AI

Every response is built from literature retrieved for that question, with the evidence shown alongside the synthesis.

  • Claims are linked to the papers behind them
  • Study design and sample size are surfaced
  • Disagreement between studies is shown, not hidden
  • Search runs against current literature
02 — Method

From question to cited answer

Five steps, run for every query — nothing is answered from memory alone.

01 Question You submit a research question in plain language — no special query syntax required.
02 Retrieval The system searches medical literature and identifies papers relevant to the question, ranked for relevance.
03 Reading Retrieved papers are parsed for findings, methodology, sample size, and stated limitations.
04 Synthesis An LLM combines the retrieved evidence into a structured answer — it does not add claims the sources don't support.
05 Citation Every claim in the final answer links back to the specific source it came from, so you can verify it directly.
03 — What you see

Conflicting results
